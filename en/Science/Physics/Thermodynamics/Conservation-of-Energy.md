# Conservation of Energy

## One-sentence definition

> The total energy of an isolated system remains constant; energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.

## Full explanation

In any process occurring in an isolated system, the sum of all forms of energy (kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electromagnetic, mass-energy, etc.) stays the same. This is one of the most fundamental and thoroughly tested principles in all of science. Einstein later showed that mass itself is a form of energy ($E = mc^2$), extending the principle.

## Feynman-style explanation

Energy is like the LEGO blocks of the universe. You always have exactly the same number of blocks. You can build a car, then take it apart and build a house, but when you count the blocks at the end of the day you still have exactly the same number. They never disappear and you can never invent new ones out of nothing.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **LEGO blocks**: Always the same number, just rearranged.
2. **Bank account**: You can move money between accounts (forms of energy), but the total balance of a closed system never changes.
3. **Water cycle**: Water evaporates, forms clouds, rains, flows in rivers — the amount of water stays roughly constant (ignoring deep geological processes).

## Concrete examples

1. Roller coaster: Gravitational potential energy at the top converts into kinetic energy at the bottom, then into heat through friction in the brakes.
2. Burning wood: Chemical energy stored in the molecular bonds is released as heat and light.
3. Nuclear power: A tiny amount of mass is converted into an enormous amount of energy according to $E = mc^2$.

## Quantitative / data anchors

- Energy is measured in joules (J).
- $1\ \text{calorie} = 4.184\ \text{J}$
- The total energy of the observable universe is estimated to be around $10^{70}$ joules (order of magnitude, depending on cosmological model).

## Historical context

Multiple scientists arrived at the idea almost simultaneously in the 1840s. Joule’s careful experiments measuring the mechanical equivalent of heat were decisive. Helmholtz gave the most general mathematical formulation.

## Relationships

### Builds on / Presupposes
- Concept of energy in its various forms

### Influences / Leads to
- Second Law of Thermodynamics (which adds the directionality)
- All of modern physics, chemistry and engineering
- Einstein’s mass-energy equivalence

### Contrasts with
- Processes that appear to create or destroy energy (they never do when the system is properly defined)

## Common misconceptions

- "Energy can be created by living beings" → No. Living systems transform energy, they do not create it.
- "When something stops moving, energy is lost" → It has usually been converted into heat (disordered kinetic energy of molecules).
