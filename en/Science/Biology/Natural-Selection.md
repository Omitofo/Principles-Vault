# Natural Selection

## One-sentence definition

> Organisms with heritable traits that better suit them to their environment tend to survive and reproduce more successfully, causing those traits to become more common in successive generations.

## Full explanation

Natural selection is the differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to differences in phenotype. It is the primary mechanism of adaptive evolution. For natural selection to occur, three conditions are necessary: variation in traits, heritability of those traits, and differential reproductive success correlated with those traits.

## Feynman-style explanation

Imagine a group of bears in the snow. The ones born with white fur hide better and catch more food. The dark ones are easily seen by their prey and go hungry. Over many generations, almost all the bears end up white because the white ones had more babies who also tended to be white.

## Key metaphors & analogies

1. **Coffee filter**: The environment acts like a filter that only lets certain traits pass to the next generation.
2. **Sieve**: Variation is poured in; only the variants that fit the current environment pass through.
3. **Trial and error on a massive scale**: Nature tries many slight variations; the ones that work better are kept.

## Concrete examples

1. Peppered moths in industrial England: Dark moths became more common when tree bark was blackened by pollution.
2. Antibiotic resistance in bacteria: Bacteria with resistance mutations survive and multiply rapidly when antibiotics are present.
3. Darwin’s finches: Beak shapes changed over generations according to the available food sources on different islands.

## Quantitative / data anchors

- Selection can produce measurable changes in allele frequencies in just a few generations (especially in organisms with short generation times).
- The strength of selection is often measured by the selection coefficient $s$.

## Historical context

Independently conceived by Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace. Darwin published *On the Origin of Species* in 1859. The modern understanding incorporates genetics (the Modern Synthesis of the 1930s–40s).

## Common misconceptions

- "Natural selection is random" → The variation is random (or undirected), but the selection process is not.
- "Natural selection has a goal or direction toward perfection" → It only favors what works better in the current environment.
- "Individuals evolve" → Populations evolve; individuals are selected.
